# Impact Formula: source-grounded framework
## Andrés / Yosh transcripts → Jason's Apohenia Sales OS
Prepared September 10, 2026. This is a source study and design specification, not a claim that the application has been implemented or that a script guarantees sales.

## How to read this package

**Source A** is the uploaded brain-dump lesson followed by a reviewed Yosh sales conversation. **Source B** is the longer multi-instructor course. Exact originals are in `sources/`; editorial section boundaries, character offsets and SHA-256 hashes are in `data/`. Both uploaded texts are single-line, untimed transcripts. Video-to-transcript pairing has not been independently established. No audio, body language, screen-only slides, or payment event has been verified by inspecting video. Delivery instructions below are what the instructors describe in the text.

There are three deliberately separate layers:

1. **Source study:** what the instructors teach, including controversial or coercive material. Do not rewrite the historical record to make it sound like a different method.
2. **Normalized source questions:** templates with repeated fillers removed and industry examples abstracted. These are editorial reconstructions, not exact quotes. Each points to an unchanged source excerpt and character range.
3. **Apohenia scripts:** original, practical adaptations for Jason. These carry different approval and publication states and do not masquerade as Andrés's exact words.

The source archive captures all 1,449 literal question marks: 527 in A and 922 in B. This is an exhaustive punctuation audit, not 1,449 unique sales questions. It includes teacher questions, rhetorical questions, quoted speech, repeated variants and questions from the reviewed call. Some spoken questions lack a question mark; the curated bank includes normalized examples of these, but punctuation alone cannot certify semantic completeness. The entire original wording remains available. There are 207 curated source-derived question records, including 48 records from the reviewed call. Some records group adjacent questions with the same purpose.

## 1. The actual core framework

Both sources name four phases: **Intent → Logical Certainty → Emotional Certainty → Pitch**. The setting call normally stops after logical certainty and an appointment-setting transition. The closer continues through emotional certainty and the pitch. Objection handling is a subsequent branching system, not a substitute for the four phases. [A01–A07; B00–B05]

The instructors' general method is: understand the purpose of a question, know what type of answer it seeks, add the prospect's context, ask in a deliberate delivery style, and do not simply move forward when the needed answer has not been obtained. They also explicitly teach exact script internalization. In A12, the instructor uses instant recall of the third logical-certainty question as an illustration: knowing the sequence frees attention for the prospect. In B08, adaptation is described as stage goal → answer desired → context → wording. Neither principle requires replacing actual word tracks with abstract headings. [A09, A12; B08–B09]

### Terminology

| Source term | Meaning in these transcripts | Important distinction for the build |
|---|---|---|
| Tangible | A desired future result / overarching goal. Later, concrete future outcomes are also called tangibles. | Store an initial goal separately from later, detailed future scenes. |
| Experience / past experience | A past or present problem underlying that goal. | A second statement of the same goal is not automatically an experience. |
| Current process / strategy / regime | How the prospect currently tries to achieve the goal. | Record what is known; a causal connection to the problem is not established merely by proximity in conversation. |
| Gap | Difference between present circumstances and desired result. | Distinguish verified quantities, prospect estimates, hypotheses and aspirations. |
| Probe | A question that clarifies or expands a preceding answer. | Preserve the exact term being clarified. |
| Impact | A downstream effect of the problem. | Can be operational, economic or personal; it is not synonymous with emotional intensity. |
| Rationale | Why seek advanced help instead of continuing alone/current approach? | Instructor frames it as prehandling DIY. |
| What prevented / what shifted | Prior barrier and what changed now. | Do not treat a stated shift as permanent removal of a real constraint. |
| Ideal criteria | What the prospect needs to see in a new solution. | Feed only deliverable, approved matches into the pitch. |
| Mirror question | Restate the answer objective using a different question after noting the mismatch. | This is not merely repeating the prospect's last three words. |
| Consequence / cost of inaction | Imagined future if the problem remains or the desired outcome is not reached. | ASR sometimes says “cost of an action”; normalized terminology is editorial. |
| Pillar | A component/step of the offer tied to the customer's problem and desired result. | A pillar name is not proof of a deliverable or an outcome. |
| Agreeance statement | Broad agreement intended to reframe an objection into certainty or perspective. | Source term, not an established statement that every objection is actually fear. |
| Success point | A point in the client journey where the client experiences progress and confidence. | Milestones in examples are not universal calendar rules. |
| UVP | Unique value proposition of a higher-tier offer. | B21 organizes it as more, better/more efficient, or more likely to achieve the new result. |

## 2. Entry routes and role boundaries

**Warm setter:** The source starts from a genuine lead action: an ad opt-in, downloaded/purchased resource, webinar attendance, incomplete call booking, or requested contact. Confirm identity and the actual action. Ask intent and experience; complete logical certainty; assess next-step relevance and schedule or nurture. [A01–A03; B02]

**Direct booked closer without complete discovery:** A04 distinguishes new-activity intent from improvement-specific intent. Ask one appropriate opener, not both automatically. Continue through experience, logical certainty, emotional certainty and pitch.

**Closer with completed setter discovery:** Recap the setter's notes, obtain confirmation, then begin emotional certainty. That is how the source avoids repeating the setting call. Apohenia adds an evidence check: missing or disputed notes route to the missing question, not blindly past discovery.

**True cold outbound:** The source materials discuss outbound activity, but their fully explained opening presumes prior interest. They do not supply a complete permission-first cold dealership script. The cold opening, gatekeeper route, relevant reason for contact, opt-out route and short-meeting alternative in our scripts are **Apohenia additions**, not verbatim source training.

**Upsell:** Separate source variants for setting and closing; never merely replay the original problem as though delivered results did not happen. [B20–B21]

## 3. Intent: exact answer objectives

Core sequence (I01–I08): confirm person → confirm actual lead context → select one intent question → experience question. For a new activity, ask what they hope to obtain by exploring it. For improvement, ask what they specifically need help with. Then ask what they have seen that makes them believe the gap exists. [A01, A04; B01–B02]

**Source distinction:** “I want more money” is a tangible. “I cannot afford the trips I want / my present income is capped” is the kind of experience the source seeks. An answer can be brief and still satisfy the purpose. A long biography may not satisfy it.

**Source mirror:** When experience produces another generic future goal, the instructor switches to what the goal would let the prospect do that they cannot do now. This is a different-looking question seeking the same gap information. [A09; B09]

**Production rule:** Desired answer *type* is known; the prospect's actual answer is not predetermined. “I do not have that problem” remains an answer, not an invitation to manufacture a confession.

## 4. Logical Certainty: process → positives → problem → impact

The repeatable sequence is L01–L14:

| Order | Source question purpose | Information to keep |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What are you doing now to achieve the goal? | Current process, tools and actions. |
| 2 | How long have you done it that way? | Process duration. |
| 3 | What caused you to choose it? | Origin/rationale; taught also as a doubt question. |
| 4 | Besides the unmet result, do you like the approach? | Satisfaction. |
| 5 | What do you like? | Valued elements. |
| 6 | What would you change about the process or results? | Specific problem. |
| 7 | What do you mean? | Definition/example. |
| 8 | How long has that problem occurred? | Problem duration, not necessarily process duration. |
| 9 | Has it affected the relevant downstream outcome? How? | Impact. |

The instructor's stated mechanism is associating the current process with the problem so the person becomes more open to a different approach. The source says to avoid small talk that dissipates this focus. It also prescribes asking about positives so the seller appears unbiased, including “forcing” a no-response back to a positive answer. The production adaptation asks what should be preserved but accepts that there may be nothing the customer likes. This change is explicit, not an alternative account of the source. [A02; B01–B02]

Impact is industry-dependent: the fitness examples probe motivation and energy; marketing examples probe enough work to hire a crew; business-opportunity examples ask about day-to-day life. In A13, the reviewed call instead quantifies sales volume, conversion and estimated missed commissions. That is a source example of using the same purpose with different wording.

## 5. Setter transition

The money-offer example asks a six-month target, current position/gap, sometimes willingness to invest, and permission to connect to a closer who can investigate more deeply. A03 makes the spend-willingness question conditional when the income answer looks unqualified. B02 presents it more generally. Neither a target income nor theoretical willingness establishes actual affordability. [S01–S05]

B17 adds a **nurture-first** alternative when the person barely knows the company: send a specifically relevant resource, agree on a reconnection in roughly two or three days, then ask whether they remain interested, reviewed it, what they took away and how it applies. The course describes testimonial material disguised as training and teasing someone who did not watch; Apohenia labels testimonial material accurately and offers rescheduling without guilt. [U01–U06]

## 6. Emotional Certainty, first half: decision history

The source rationale question asks why the person seeks a more advanced solution instead of doubling down on their existing process or solving it alone. The source often contrasts advanced help positively with “the average person” negatively. That is a deliberate rhetorical contrast in the teaching, not an objective finding that alternatives are bad. [E01]

Then the **A05 decision tree** is:

```
Were you looking for other solutions before we spoke?
├─ No → What prevented you? → What shifted now?
└─ Yes → Did you move forward with anything?
   ├─ No → What prevented you? → What shifted now?
   └─ Yes → What result did you get?
      ├─ Bad → What would you need to see this time? (ideal criteria)
      └─ Good → Why seek something else now? (remaining bottleneck)
```

The claimed source objective is to discover and “prehandle” the reason likely to return as an objection. B03 emphasizes personal action/investment rather than merely consuming free content. A13 shows a real variation: when a prospect supplies a plausible reason for the timing of their return to sales, the closer moves to ideal criteria rather than continually insisting on a different reason. [E02–E08; V26–V31]

**Production additions:** mixed result, ongoing provider, unknown, genuinely no prior need, still constrained, not authorized, and declined-to-answer branches. These are not presented as branches explicitly diagrammed by the source. Prior answers can change. A buyer retains the right to revise a decision.

## 7. Optional identity frame: after “what shifted”

B10 gives a longer optional frame between shifted and future pacing. Its structure is wanting a result versus doing what it takes; identify target and baseline; contrast present mindset and skill/process with the desired future; ask whether that approach is worth keeping, why, what happens if it stays, and commitment to becoming the person who can achieve the goal. It then flows to what achieving the goal would allow. [D06–D11]

A12 separately teaches identity reinforcement based on past/present actions and imagined future outcomes. General labels are **intelligent, bold, courageous**; additional role labels include father/provider/employer. Casual acknowledgments and planned “kudos / unlike most people / kudos” contrasts are given. Negative future examples deliberately attach regret or failure to the person's worth as a parent or person. The source archive retains this; commercial scripts do not make purchase a test of being a good parent or worthy person.

Jason's own identity interview is a different use case: **he chooses and revises his standards for himself**. It must not secretly use a sales pressure sequence against him or export his personal priorities into every prospect's profile.

## 8. Emotional Certainty, second half: future → meaning → consequence

Positive future: ask what would tangibly change after solving the problem. Seek two or three specific outcomes. Follow a vague word with details: which situation, what location, with whom, what practical difference, what amount or time frame if relevant. For B2B, the instructor first discusses the business and then the owner. Personal probing is not mandatory when it is irrelevant or declined. [F03–F12; B03]

**Important source wording:** the term must mean something to this person. A13 distinguishes the customer's word “gratification” from a generic synonym and asks what it means. The same call has the closer propose “efficiency,” which the prospect explicitly repeats/accepts. These are two different provenance cases: a prospect-originated term and a seller-originated label confirmed by the prospect. Our vocabulary module retains that distinction. [V25, V43]

Consequence: the instructor switches from positive future to what happens if the current trajectory persists. The repeated “two days, two weeks, two months, even two years” wording extends the time horizon. A06 seeks two or three specific negative outcomes and may skip the extra feeling question in B2B. B03 describes one or two and adds a day-to-day-feeling question at three, six and twelve months, explicitly aiming for “regret.” [C01–C06]

The instructors deliberately aim for a close temporal contrast between high positive and low negative emotion to create urgency. That is the source's stated persuasion mechanism. The production system instead records an accurate comparison of options and stated consequences; it must not score “how distressed did you make them?” as a success metric.

## 9. Commitment and three-pillar pitch

The source commitment sequence is: unwillingness to settle → why change now → whose responsibility. B03 notes that the responsibility question may be inappropriate for some individual business owners. Next is permission to put together a game plan. [P01–P04]

For each of three pillars:

**Name the pillar → connect to their previously stated problem → explain the relevant delivery/solution → check relevance or understanding.**

B04 recommends comprehensible, result-oriented pillar names, rather than jargon. The examples are portal/structure, coaching/accountability and ongoing consistency/support. These are features of the example course, NOT Apohenia's actual offer.

After pillars, ask perceived fit, why, the most helpful part, and sometimes what that part means personally. Then quote the real price and ask how to proceed. **A07 presents price after pillars; B04 explicitly allows price first and says that instructor prefers it.** Both variants belong in the library. Lock one approved variant during a particular practice/cohort; do not silently alternate while Jason is memorizing it.

Source 3–5 minute pitch guidance is a teaching heuristic, not a reason to omit scope, costs, limitations or terms. A specific buyer's comprehension matters more than meeting a stopwatch target.

## 10. Objection handling: preserve the source tree, do not call it universal truth

The source always starts with money logistics, regardless of the first objection. It argues that objections have a money component. It isolates value with a hypothetical gift of the full price, asks why and personal impact, then considers payment timing and a plan that fits budget. After renewed decision request, branches are buy, time/partner logistics or fear. [O01–O08; A11; B05]

**Time:** value without time constraint → two-person analogy (same responsibilities as excuse versus reason) → ask which identity they choose and why → consequence of the first choice → commitment/action. [O08–O12]

**Partner:** value with imagined partner approval → what if partner declines → crown/responsibility analogy → who does the work / whose responsibility / fair to delegate / why → consequence → action. The source treats continued partner consultation after responsibility agreement as fear. That is the instructor's interpretation; it is not proof of actual signing authority or joint-funds permission. [O13–O21]

**Fear:** an “agreeance statement” reframes the concern into seeking certainty, or into perspective. Then use **Frame → Pushback → Consequence → CTA**. The CTA can target identity, mindset or buying. [O22–O31]

**Worked analogies actually present:** A11 gives worker-with-stable-pay versus entrepreneur uncertainty; B05 gives going to the beach without perfect weather certainty. Time's two-people and partner's crown frames are also worked through. The optional runner/lion analogy is a separate future-pace setup, not one of the complete four fear scripts.

**Missing material:** the instructors refer to four fear reframes but do not spell out every member of one complete four-frame set. A names perspective frames including island, what's riskier, fat person and $4,000; B pacing includes fragments of island language, not the complete script. The archive marks these as named/partial, not reconstructed. Do not fill the gap from imagination and attribute it to Andrés or Yosh.

**Exit:** the source recommends ending after four reframes rather than spending hours chasing a small payment. The example apology suggests the seller failed the prospect and that the prospect will suffer. Apohenia instead uses a respectful no-sale exit and only an agreed follow-up. There is no minimum of four attempts after a clear refusal.

**Commercial adaptation:** diagnose the actual constraint first, retain budget/time/authority/fit as independent possible blockers, explain approved proof and alternatives, and stage the commitment so risk is manageable. Do not turn every no into fear or remove the no-sale exit.

## 11. Delivery: versioned instructor cues

| Element | A / Andrés-described emphasis | B / Yosh-described emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Casual | Open posture, visible hands; most of call; confident pitch. | Conversational familiarity, individual natural vocabulary, not adopting another personality. |
| Curious | Tilt head, squint; early discovery; positive future with upward inflection. | Intent/logical certainty; lower guard; first-person demonstration. |
| Skeptical | Lean back, brow cue; some logical questions and shifted. | Mainly first half of emotional certainty; leaning out; some described examples differ. |
| Concern/empathy | Lean toward, raised eyebrows; optional chest/heart gesture. | Slow pacing; instructor specifically dislikes the hand-on-heart gesture for himself. |
| Pacing | Cues can accelerate/decelerate and follow emotional direction. | Generally faster early discovery, slower emotional sections; mixed pacing for reframe/pushback/consequence/CTA. |

These are instructor-described performance cues, not measured physiology or an audio evaluation of the videos. Audio-only prospects cannot see gesture; a gesture can still be offered as an optional practice cue for Jason's delivery, but do not score whether the prospect saw it.

B16 has an internal wording inconsistency near its final parenthetical: it gives “slow pacing … or fast pacing” for early phases after teaching faster intent/logical. Preserve that as a transcript ambiguity; do not assert the text specifies one unambiguous exact rhythm everywhere.

**Verbal cueing:** choose five acknowledgments that fit Jason, e.g. “Right,” “Okay,” “I see,” “That makes sense,” “Go on.” These are proposed defaults, not his selected preferences. Source examples use yeah/really/okay/right/damn. Practice pace, space and inflection without continuously interrupting. The interjection bridge is acknowledgment → refer to what they said → next question. [A08; B13–B16]

## 12. Three psychological taxonomies, not one score

| Taxonomy in source | Categories | Intended source use |
|---|---|---|
| Six human needs | Certainty; uncertainty; love/connection; growth; significance; contribution. | Understand which motivations the instructor believes matter most; he attributes lens to Tony Robbins. |
| Alpha/beta buying pocket | Assertive/high-confidence versus low-confidence patterns; desired middle “buying pocket.” | Source recommends raising or lowering perceived confidence/status. |
| Reinforced identities | Intelligent; bold; courageous; role-specific father/provider/employer etc. | Source labels to influence self-perception and commitment. |

None of these is a complete catalogue of the ego archetypes Jason mentioned. “Approval,” “intelligence” and other user examples must not be invented as extra members of the source's six human needs. In production, record user-confirmed decision preferences with quotations, uncertainty and corrections—not hidden diagnoses or permanent personality tags.

The source's status-deflation examples deliberately mishear a number to make it seem smaller and compare a buyer to an unstated higher standard. Retain them in source study, not in live sales coaching. A factual comparison requires a real benchmark and relevance to the prospect.

## 13. Follow-ups, referrals, upsells: the complete ecosystem

**Setter follow-up:** relevant resource → agreed reconnect → current interest → watched → takeaway → application → standard appointment transition. [B17; U01–U06]

**Closer follow-up:** B18 proposes 1/2/4/6/8-week buckets, a daily 30-minute block, approximately two touches a week, and different content for “problem unaware” versus “solution unaware.” These are course prescriptions; not a universal legal contact cadence or proof that all prospects fit those two types. Apohenia also records budget, implementation, authority, deliberate deferral, bad fit and do-not-contact. The buckets represent a next expected step, not permission to move agreed dates earlier without consent. [U07–U09]

**Referrals:** ask at actual success points, understand who might benefit and why, then seek an introduction. The dates day 1/10/35/60/75 are explicitly examples tied to an offer journey. Production checks satisfaction and actual progress, accepts no, and keeps referred contacts out of automatic campaigns until appropriately reviewed. [B19; R01–R07]

**Upsell setter, five steps:** feedback/results → new goal → remaining problem and probes → diagnostic expansion → transition to advanced help/closer. Expansion can be a role-play or review of real operational numbers. Source recommends pretending to be customer support and sometimes implying credit/no extra money; these claims are not transferred to the live script. [B20; X01–X11]

**Upsell closer:** review actual positives → new tangible → why important now → two/three future specifics → personal meaning → consequence of not reaching the new goal → permission → previous-versus-new offer comparison → fit/why/key pillar → decision. The new offer must have genuine additional value, not charge to repair an original unfulfilled promise. [B21; Y01–Y11]

**UVP:** more of something useful; better/more efficient delivery; or evidence that makes the desired result more likely. No “more likely” claim without adequate basis. Source revenue-share and conversion targets vary between modules, so they are examples or proposed teaching targets, not a coherent measured benchmark dataset.

## 14. Reviewed call: what it demonstrates and what it does not

A13 follows a life-insurance salesperson moving from another sales setting. It demonstrates vague intent → mirror; current process; metrics; pivot from self-described tone issues to concrete objections; hypothetical missed opportunity; target and history; previous training; criteria; specific personal goals; vocabulary probing; consequence; commitment; pitch transition. [V01–V48]

Two particularly important examples for the product are **efficiency** (seller-proposed, prospect-confirmed label) and **gratification** (prospect's own word, clarified rather than swapped for satisfaction).

The prospect's estimates of extra convertible sales are hypotheses, not an independently verified revenue loss. The conversation also mixes skill progress and income progress, which are not the same denominator. The source's narrator says it closed for $10,000 paid in full, but the supplied excerpt does not show the full pitch or payment. Mark narrator-claimed outcome separately from observed transcript events. It is not evidence for Apohenia's effectiveness.

## 15. Training and personal identity content

B06 teaches process orientation, repetition, one feedback focus at a time and activity rather than endless passive study. B07 adds calls, pipeline/follow-up, admin and referrals. B09's mirror duel alternates different questions seeking the same answer type until someone runs out. A12's script internalization gives a direct rationale for Jason's memorization requirement.

B24 distinguishes who the seller is and the skills they developed from money or the company they work for. It warns against replacing a working process after a small run of declined calls. These are relevant inputs to Jason's self-directed interview and evidence journal, without treating one recorded sale as proof of his worth.

B22–B25 also contain claims about physiology/anchors, sleep, isolation and burnout. These have been preserved in the source copy and indexed, but **have not been independently validated and are not health guidance or mandatory operating standards**. The app will not prescribe sleep deprivation, fast-breathing routines, isolation from supportive relationships, or denial of exhaustion. User-selected readiness, ordinary breaks and sustainable practice are production-design choices, not claims extracted from those passages.

## 17. The resulting build objective

**Self-directed identity interview → approved offer definition → exact script version → memorization and branch drills → human-led calling with prominent language reminders → evidence-grounded review → one targeted repetition → careful version improvement.**

The first app milestone must already let Jason answer the interview by clicking, read and rehearse a complete script, select simulated responses, practice mirrors, pin words and review a drill. Calling integrations follow that working core. The interface should teach the source accurately while helping Jason develop his own approved commercial style.
